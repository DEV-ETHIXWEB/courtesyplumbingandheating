# DIY Tips for Preventing Clogged Drains: Do’s & Don’ts

When it comes to maintaining your home’s plumbing system, preventing clogs in your drains is one of the most effective ways to avoid costly repairs and ensure smooth operation. In Castle Rock, Colorado, where weather conditions can add extra strain on plumbing systems, knowing the right preventive measures is essential. Courtesy Plumbing & Heating is here to share practical DIY tips for keeping your drains clear and trouble-free.

### The Do’s of Preventing Clogged Drains

- **Do Use Drain Screens**: Install drain screens over sinks, showers, and bathtub drains. These simple mesh covers catch hair, food particles, and other debris that can accumulate and form clogs over time.

- **Do Flush Drains with Hot Water**: Pouring hot water down your drains periodically helps dissolve soap scum and other substances that build up on the walls of pipes.

- **Do Clean Stoppers and Strainers**: Regularly remove and clean stoppers in sinks and bathtubs to eliminate hair and residue. This habit helps prevent blockages before they start.

- **Do Dispose of Grease Properly**: After cooking, let grease cool and pour it into a disposable container instead of down the drain. Grease hardens and can create stubborn clogs.

- **Do Use Natural Drain Cleaners**: A simple mix of baking soda and vinegar can work wonders to break down minor blockages and keep your drains fresh. Pour half a cup of baking soda down the drain, followed by half a cup of vinegar. Wait 15 minutes, then flush with hot water.

### The Don’ts of Preventing Clogged Drains

- **Don’t Use Chemical Drain Cleaners Regularly**: While chemical drain cleaners can be effective in emergencies, their harsh ingredients can erode your pipes over time and harm the environment. Limit their use and rely on safer methods for regular maintenance.

- **Don’t Rinse Food Scraps Down the Kitchen Sink**: Even if you have a garbage disposal, fibrous foods like celery, coffee grounds, and potato peels can wrap around the blades or settle in the pipes, leading to blockages.

- **Don’t Pour Leftover Paint or Cleaning Solutions Down Drains**: These substances can coat pipes and create a buildup that contributes to slow drainage and future clogs.

- **Don’t Ignore Early Signs of Clogs**: If water starts to drain slowly or you hear gurgling sounds from your pipes, take action promptly. Ignoring these warning signs can lead to more significant issues that require professional intervention.

- **Don’t Forget About Outdoor Drains**: Leaves, twigs, and other yard debris can obstruct outdoor drains and cause water to back up into your home. Regularly inspect and clear these areas, especially during fall.
